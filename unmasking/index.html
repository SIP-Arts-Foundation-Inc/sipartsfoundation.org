Unmasking in the MetroEast

Unmasking in the MetroEast is an educational program that results in a community arts showcase.

What it teaches is masking: hiding, changing, or holding back parts of ourselves to fit expectations, avoid judgment, or feel safe. As AANE explains it: "Masking, also called camouflaging, is something many Autistic people do to fit in with societal expectations. It means changing, hiding, or copying behaviors to blend into neurotypical environments."

Autistic masking is where this starts. It is not where it stops. Anyone who has held part of themselves back has something true to say about it, and those experiences are not the same weight or the same shape.

Everyone learns first, then answers in their own work. The program runs on two tracks: the Ausome Expressions residency cohort of thirty, across ten Saturdays, and the whole Metro East, on discarded political yard signs that SIP collects, cleans, and gives back out as free art material. A yard sign is what a candidate used to show the best possible version of themselves. Cleaned and handed back blank, it becomes where you show something real.

Both tracks hang in the same room on Saturday, April 3, 2027, 10am to 2pm, at A Special Place Campus in Belleville, held in celebration of World Autism Awareness Day. Free and open to everyone.

After every election, thousands of yard signs come down and go straight to the landfill. We are collecting them instead, cleaning them, and handing them back to our neighbors as free art panels.

What divided a yard can decorate a wall.

The prompt

What do you show the world—and what do you wish the world could see?

Transform a yard sign using colors, symbols, textures, or words to express what unmasking means to you. Share only what feels comfortable.

Need inspiration?

Optional starting points. The prompt above is the one that matters, and nothing here is collected or submitted anywhere.

  • Something people misunderstand about me…
  • A part of myself I hold back…
  • I feel accepted when…
  • If I felt free to be myself…

What we are actually doing

A sign that spent the fall telling your neighbors how to vote becomes a blank panel, and then becomes your answer to the prompt above. Three stages, one sign.

  • Step one A sign nobody needs anymore, headed for the landfill.
  • Step two We wash and prime it. Now it is a free blank panel.
  • Step three You answer the prompt on it: what you show, and what you wish was seen.

How this connects to Ausome Expressions

Unmasking in the MetroEast has two halves, and they are one effort rather than two separate projects.

  • The community art initiativeAnyone in the Metro East can take a free panel, answer the prompt, and send it back. That is everything described above. No cohort, no commitment, no cost.
  • Ausome ExpressionsA free artist residency for thirty young people. Ten Saturdays, noon to 3, January 23 through March 27, 2027. The cohort studies masking in depth and builds the April 3 showcase itself: the work on the walls, the layout of the room, and the running of the day.

So the community makes the panels, and the cohort makes the show. Every panel that comes back, from a family at a kitchen table or a scout troop in one session, is displayed in the space the cohort designed.

Joining the residency

  • Three pathways, ten people in each. Visual and Written, Music and Dance, or Design and Production. You pick one.
  • About 30 documented service hours across the ten Saturdays, which counts toward school and scholarship service requirements.
  • Free. No tuition, no materials cost.
  • Applications open December 15, 2026 and close January 15, 2027.
  • Attend at least 9 of the 10 sessions and your place at The Arc of Illinois conference in the Chicago area is covered.

Ausome Expressions is a cohort of SIP’s MetroEast Career Academy. To apply, or to ask whether it is a fit, email unmasking@sipartsfoundation.org.

Why “unmasking”

Masking is what a lot of autistic and disabled people do every day to seem acceptable: holding still, holding back, performing a version of themselves that costs them something to maintain. Plenty of people who are not autistic recognize some of that too, in a workplace, a classroom, or a family. The weight is not the same, and this project does not pretend it is. A yard sign is a mask too. It is the loudest, flattest version of what a person believes, planted on a lawn, speaking for a whole household.

We take the flattest thing in the yard and hand it to someone who will put something true on it. Then we put all of it in one room, side by side, and let people look.

Why this is education, not decoration

Masking is one of the least understood parts of being autistic, and it is rarely taught anywhere. Every group that takes a panel learns what masking is first, then answers it in their own work. The art is how we know the learning landed.

Starting from autistic masking and letting everyone answer it is deliberate. Acceptance is built by people making something together, not by an audience being lectured at.

  • The learning goal: every participant can explain what masking is, why people do it, and what it costs.
  • How we know it worked: two short questions before participants start and two after they finish, so we can show what changed in people's understanding.
  • Who it reaches: classrooms, churches, scout troops, libraries, day programs, and families across our nine counties, at no cost to any of them.
  • The environmental piece: every panel is a plastic yard sign kept out of a Metro East landfill.

Questions people ask

Who can participate?

Anyone in the Metro East. Families, individual artists of any age or skill level, classrooms, art teachers, school clubs, churches, scout troops, libraries, day programs, community groups, and campaigns clearing out signs.

You do not have to be autistic, disabled, or connected to SIP in any way. You do not need art experience. There is no cost at any step.

What materials do I need?

We supply the panel, washed and primed and ready to work on. Everything after that is up to you: paint, marker, pencil, collage, fabric, paper, tape, words. Anything that sticks to a primed plastic panel will work.

Acrylic paint and paint markers hold best on this surface. If supplies are the thing standing in your way, say so when you ask for a panel and we will work it out.

How do I request panels?

Use the form below and say in the notes box how many panels you need and roughly how many people are taking part. Groups get a packet with a short lesson outline and a group code printed on everything.

You can request a panel any time up to March 8, 2027, one week before artwork is due back. Sign collection ends December 31, but we hold prepped panels for the whole project.

You can also email unmasking@sipartsfoundation.org and we will set it up with you directly.

How do I return finished artwork?

Artwork is due March 15, 2027. Bring it to SIP Arts Foundation, 424 Lebanon Avenue, Belleville, IL 62220, with your participant code or group code written on the back.

Individuals fill out the second survey with the same code they got at the start. Group leaders fill out one leader form with their group totals instead.

What accessibility support is available?

Adapted tools, grips, and seated work setups are available, and we will arrange what a participant needs rather than asking them to work around it. Ask when you request a panel.

The April 3 showcase is held in a sensory-friendly space with a quiet area available throughout. If you need something specific to take part or to attend, email unmasking@sipartsfoundation.org and tell us what would help.

Do I have to share something personal?

No. The prompt invites you to go as deep as you want and no further, and the instruction on it is deliberate: share only what feels comfortable.

An abstract panel, a pattern, a color study, or a single word is a complete answer. The optional sentence about your piece and the name on it are both optional, and the name defaults to Anonymous.
