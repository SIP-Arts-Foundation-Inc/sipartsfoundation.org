A community art project

Unmasking in the MetroEast

After every election, thousands of yard signs come down and go straight to the landfill. We are collecting them instead, cleaning them, and handing them back to our neighbors as free art panels.

What divided a yard can decorate a wall.

Corrugated plastic signs are not accepted in most curbside recycling. They are layered plastic with wire stakes, so they sit in a landfill for decades. Every sign you hand us is one less in the ground and one more blank canvas in a child's hands.

What we are actually doing

A sign that spent the fall telling your neighbors how to vote becomes a blank panel, and then becomes a piece of art about what it costs to hide who you are.

  • Step one A sign nobody needs anymore, headed for the landfill.
  • Step two We wash and prime it. Now it is a free blank panel.
  • Step three You answer the theme on it. The mask comes off.

How it works

  1. You bring them byLeftover or discarded yard signs, stakes included. Muddy, bent, or faded is fine. Any candidate, any issue, any year.
  2. We clean and primeSigns are washed, dried, and primed into blank panels ready to paint.
  3. The community createsFamilies, students, classrooms, church groups, and community organizations take panels for free and make art about what it costs to hide who you are, and what it feels like to stop.
  4. We show the workEvery panel comes back together for a free public showcase on April 3, 2027.

Learn what masking is

Every panel starts with learning. Take the short survey first, then pick one thing to read or watch, then make your art. The survey takes about two minutes and is how we know whether this project actually taught the community something.

On your own

For individuals, families, and artists of any age.

  1. Take the first survey

    Answer before you read anything. It is fine, and useful, to say you have never heard the word. That is the starting point we measure from. You will get a participant code to keep.

    Start the survey
  2. Pick one and learn

    Choose whichever fits you best. One is enough.

    • Masking, National Autistic SocietyShort and plain language. Start here if you want the quickest explanation.
    • Masking: A Guide, AANEWritten by and for autistic adults. Best on what unmasking actually feels like.
    • Autistic Masking Explained, Neurodivergent InsightsMore visual, with diagrams. Good if you want a little more depth.
  3. Make your panel
    Your promptWhat does unmasking in the Metro East look like to you?

    Paint, draw, collage, write, or stick things on. There is no wrong answer and no skill level required.

  4. Bring it back with the second survey

    Artwork is due March 15, 2027. The second survey comes back with your panel and uses the same code, so we can see what changed.

With a group

For anyone running this with a group: classroom teachers, scout troop leaders, church and youth leaders, club advisors, day programs, and libraries.

  1. Request your panels

    Tell us how many you need and roughly how many people are taking part. We send a group packet with your group code already printed on everything.

    Request panels for a group
  2. Run it in one session

    Your packet has a short lesson outline, the three links above, and paper before-and-after half sheets for your group. We collect no names and no personal information about anyone in your group.

  3. Report once, for everyone

    You fill out one leader form with your group totals instead of each person filling out two surveys. One form, one group code, done.

  4. Send the panels back

    Artwork is due March 15, 2027, and every piece is shown at the free public showcase on April 3, 2027. Your group is invited.

The three links above go to outside organizations. We share them because they explain masking well, and we check them regularly.

Why “unmasking”

Masking is what a lot of autistic and disabled people do every day to seem acceptable: holding still, holding back, performing a version of themselves that costs them something to maintain. A yard sign is a mask too. It is the loudest, flattest version of what a person believes, planted on a lawn, speaking for a whole household.

We take the flattest thing in the yard and hand it to someone who will put something true on it. Then we put all of it in one room, side by side, and let people look.

Why this is education, not decoration

Masking is one of the least understood parts of being autistic, and it is rarely taught anywhere. Every group that takes a panel learns what masking is first, then answers it in their own work. The art is how we know the learning landed.

  • The learning goal: every participant can explain what masking is, why people do it, and what it costs.
  • How we know it worked: two short questions before participants start and two after they finish, so we can show what changed in people's understanding.
  • Who it reaches: classrooms, churches, scout troops, libraries, day programs, and families across our nine counties, at no cost to any of them.
  • The environmental piece: every panel is a plastic yard sign kept out of a Metro East landfill.

What the work can look like

Pieces made by artists in our studio programs. Panels do not have to look like any of these. Paint, marker, collage, fabric, words, anything that sticks.

Who can take part

  • Families and individual artists of any age or skill level
  • Classrooms, art teachers, and school clubs
  • Churches, scout troops, libraries, and community groups
  • Campaigns and political offices with signs to clear out
  • Anyone who has a stack of signs in the garage

No art experience needed. No cost at any step. Accommodations and adapted tools are available, just ask.
