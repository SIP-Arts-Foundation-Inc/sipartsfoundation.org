Perfectly Imperfect Arts

A monthly Show and Tell on Zoom for people who want to make things. Bring whatever you have, finished or not, started or only imagined.

Some months the hard part isn't knowing what to make. It's starting.

Perfectly Imperfect Arts is a low-pressure group for people who love making things but run into real barriers getting to the table. Nobody critiques. Nobody ranks. The name is the whole idea.

Who it's for

Neurodivergent people, and anyone facing mental health or accessibility barriers, who want a place to explore creativity and connect with others doing the same. Any experience level. If the obstacle is motivation, energy, or the weight of a blank page more often than it is skill, this group is built for you.

How a session goes

  • Welcome and check in
  • Show and Tell, where anyone who wants to share their work does
  • Open conversation about what has been getting in the way, and what has been helping
  • Each person sets one simple creative intention for the next month
  • A brief closing reflection, then we confirm the next meeting date

Sharing is always optional. Cameras are always optional. Coming just to listen is a complete way to take part.

Who leads it

Nae Perez

Neurodivergent artist, mother, and disability advocate

Nae Perez is an autistic mother, disability advocate, artist, and community volunteer. Her daughter's diagnosis helped her recognize and understand her own autistic identity.

She founded Perfectly Imperfect Arts to create an inclusive, low-pressure community where neurodivergent people, and others facing mental health or accessibility barriers, can explore creativity, connect, and support one another. She now serves as SIP Arts Foundation's peer support artist, and SIP hosts the sessions so the group has a home and room to grow.

Between sessions

Nae keeps the Perfectly Imperfect Arts community going on Facebook all month: art news, check-ins, adaptive art ideas, and work by artists with disabilities. Not sure about a Saturday yet? Start there.
