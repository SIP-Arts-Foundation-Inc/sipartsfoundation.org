About us.

Illinois-based. Community-rooted. Arts-powered.

We are an Illinois nonprofit dedicated to community education on special education law, disability rights, and advocacy for neurodivergent individuals, individuals with disabilities, and their families. We carry forward nearly two decades of Southwestern Illinois community work.

See Our Programs Get in Touch

Education, advocacy, and expression

Mission

To empower individuals with disabilities and their families through community education on special education law, disability rights, and advocacy, while amplifying voices through creative expression in the arts.

Vision

A world where every individual with a disability and every family navigating special education or disability systems is informed, empowered, and free to express themselves through the arts.

Approach

We honor family expertise. We center community voices. We use storytelling and the arts as tools for advocacy, cultural connection, and systems change.

Free to families. No diagnosis required.

Our Story

This work started in 2007, long before it had a name most people would recognize. What follows is how a group of educators, caregivers, and families built it, year by year.

2007

A door opens in Southwestern Illinois

A small group of educators and caregivers began working directly with children whose needs the systems around them were not meeting. Families of children with autism, ADHD, and developmental delays started reaching out, and the answer was always yes.

That decision, to keep showing up for children other programs turned away, is the seed of everything that followed.

2008

PTL Childcare

The work took a name. Padgett Therapeutic Learning Childcare, known to families as PTL Childcare, formalized what had been happening informally: specialized, patient care for children other providers were not equipped to serve.

PTL is where this organization learned how to build around a child instead of asking a child to fit a program.

2015

The work deepens

PTL Childcare served families across Southwestern Illinois with specialized support for children with autism, ADHD, traumatic brain injuries, and developmental delays.

Just as importantly, the team began working directly with the parents and caregivers raising them. Families kept saying the same thing: no one explains any of this to us.

2018

Joining a national network

PTL Childcare became an affiliate of the Youth Volunteer Corps, an international service-learning network known for rigorous outcomes measurement and its work with young people who are too often overlooked.

The affiliation brought training, evaluation standards, and a wider community of practice.

2019

A board, and a nonprofit

The work formalized into a nonprofit. A founding board came together around it, bringing professional expertise and lived experience in equal measure: a registered nurse, a licensed clinical social worker, special educators, and parents.

Programming has run continuously ever since.

2020

In memory of Stephen Watson

Founding board member Stephen Watson passed away in October 2020. He believed in this work before it had funding, an office, or a name most people recognized.

The foundation this organization stands on is partly his.

Today

SIP Arts Foundation

Nearly two decades of community work now operate under one nonprofit: Southern Illinois Pathways for advocacy and education, Pathways to Expression for inclusive arts, and SIP Media and Co. for publishing community voices.

Same commitment. More people carrying it.

Nearly two decades of showing up

These are the families who kept coming back. The children who grew into self-advocates. The parents who walked in with a folder of paperwork and walked out with a plan.

Every photograph here is a family who found their footing in a system that was not built for them.

Two communities. One mission.

We walk alongside individuals with developmental disabilities and the families supporting them, and alongside adults of all abilities finding their own voice.

Individuals and families

Individuals with developmental disability and their families

Whether a developmental disability has been diagnosed or is just suspected, we walk with the individual and their family through every stage, helping them understand what is happening, what is possible, and what comes next.

  • Navigation for diagnoses, services, systems, and next steps
  • Educational opportunities including IEPs, IDEA rights, and school advocacy
  • Workplace opportunities through transition planning and employment readiness
Adults, all abilities

Voices ready to be heard

We help adults of all abilities use their unique voices to express their truth, turning lived experience into advocacy, community education, and cultural change through the arts.

  • Advocacy built from personal story and lived experience
  • Creative expression through writing, recorded testimony, visual art, and media
  • Community visibility on platforms that amplify voices too often unheard

Preventing the post-school service cliff

When neurodivergent young adults and young adults with disabilities leave high school, many lose access to the supports they relied on, often with no plan in place for what comes next. Without planning, that transition can lead to isolation, caregiver stress, loss of routine, and disconnection from the community.

What can be lost

  • Adult services and benefits
  • Mental health supports
  • Transportation
  • Employment pathways
  • Community connection and daily activities

How we help families plan

  • Transition-age disability resource navigation
  • Family education and advocacy support
  • Community-based opportunities and connection
  • Person-centered planning before the crisis point

Board of Directors

SIP Arts Foundation is governed by a Board of Directors providing strategic oversight and fiduciary responsibility. A majority of our Board is independent of staff and daily operations.

Board Member

Cybersecurity Professional | Former Educator

Candice is a cybersecurity professional and former educator with more than 15 years of teaching experience and a passion for helping others learn and grow. A traumatic brain injury survivor, she has spent years bringing awareness and advocacy to the brain injury community, and she draws on her own experience with the IEP process to give families a unique perspective on the challenges they face. She brings a strong foundation in communication, leadership, and problem-solving to her work in technology and project management.

Board Member

Paul “Chris” Gholston

Retired State Supervisor | Disability Services Professional

Chris is a retired supervisor for the Missouri Department of Mental Health with over two decades of experience in group homes serving individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities. An experienced security professional who currently works with Willows Way, he is passionate about inclusion and helping people of all abilities feel accepted, supported, and educated. He is also a parent with firsthand experience advocating for his children through the special education process.

Board Member

Self-Advocacy Program Leader | College Student

He serves as Program Leader for Pathways Self-Advocates, leading the cohort as a self-advocate himself. He has interned with Saint Louis Story Stitchers, bringing a youth and self-advocate perspective to every level of the Foundation's work.

Board Member

Pastor Leta Padgett Wright

Retired Banking Manager | Care Pastor

Pastor Leta Padgett Wright has dedicated her life to the service of others, blending professional excellence with a heart for ministry. She spent over 20 years in the corporate world as an Item Processing Bank Manager before transitioning into full-time ministry with Joyce Meyer Ministries, where she served in World Missions and traveled the globe as a speaker. Board Member

Maria is a Licensed Professional Counselor and doctoral trainee in Counselor Education and Supervision, with extensive experience supporting individuals with disabilities and complex mental health needs across clinical, residential, and community-based settings. Her background spans direct service, behavioral support, and structured skill-building for individuals with cognitive, emotional, developmental, and psychiatric disabilities. She serves as Board Sponsor for the Self-Advocacy Committee and is committed to strengths-based, accessible, person-centered support.

Board Member

Special Education Teacher | Life Insurance Broker & Retirement and Protection Specialist

Michelle “Chelley” Dallas-Scott is a certified special education teacher pursuing an Ed.S. in Special Education Curriculum and Instruction. Family Support Advocate

Executive Director
