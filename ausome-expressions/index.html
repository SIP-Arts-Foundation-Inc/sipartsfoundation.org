An artist residency

Ausome Expressions

A free ten-week residency for thirty young people in the Metro East. You study masking, you make work about it, and you build the public showcase that closes it.

Applications

December 15, 2026 to January 15, 2027

This is a cohort with a fixed start. Applications close January 15 and the residency begins January 23. There is no joining partway through.

Want to make art for the showcase without joining the cohort? That is a separate, open invitation with its own deadline, and you can take part any time before March 8, 2027. See Unmasking in the MetroEast.

What it is

Ausome Expressions is the artist residency inside Unmasking in the MetroEast. Thirty young people meet ten Saturdays, study what masking is and what it costs, and answer it in their own work.

The cohort also builds the showcase itself: the work on the walls, the layout of the room, and the running of the day. The whole Metro East is invited to that showcase, and what they walk into is what this cohort made.

The program is open to everyone. Participants do not need to be autistic or neurodivergent to take part. That is deliberate. Acceptance is built by people making something together, not by an audience being lectured at.

The three pathways

You pick one. Ten people in each.

  • Ten places

    Visual and Written

    Drawing, painting, collage, mixed media, poetry, essays, spoken word, storytelling.

  • Ten places

    Music and Dance

    Composing, playing, singing, choreography, movement.

  • Ten places

    Design and Production

    Building the showcase space, then scheduling and running the event itself. For people who would rather use tools than perform. The finished piece is the room working and the day going well, which is a real discipline and a real career.

What a Saturday looks like

Three hours, noon to 3. Each session opens with the week’s subject: a person with autism, their life and work, and the traits the world tends to misread. The rest is studio time, building on that subject in your own pathway.

A pathway leader works alongside your group and a teaching artist moves between all three. Work accumulates across the ten weeks, so by late March each participant has something finished and ready to show.

What you get

  • About 30 documented service hours across the ten Saturdays, which counts toward school and scholarship service requirements.
  • No cost. No tuition and no materials fee.
  • Your work shown publicly on April 3, in a room you helped build.
  • The Arc of Illinois conference, covered, in the Chicago area, for anyone who attends at least 9 of the 10 sessions.

Who it is for

  • Young people in Madison and St. Clair Counties
  • No art training required and no prior experience with SIP needed
  • Autistic, neurodivergent, and neurotypical participants together, on purpose

Accommodations and adapted tools are available, just ask. The space is sensory-friendly with a quiet area available throughout every session.

Access

  • Transportation support is available. Transportation is the barrier families name first, so we budget for it rather than treating it as someone else’s problem.
  • Light snacks at every session. Participants bring their own lunch.
